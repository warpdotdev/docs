Factory permissions control what people can do in a factory. Give teammates the User role, shown as **Factory user** in role controls, to run work without changing the configuration. Give them Editor to configure agents, repositories, and automations.

Role management requires an Enterprise plan. On other plans, team members have Editor access. Team and workspace admins and owners have Admin access.

## Roles and capabilities

Editor includes the User capabilities. Admin includes the Editor capabilities.

| Action | User | Editor | Admin |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| View the factory and run work | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Edit the factory's name, handle, and definition | No | Yes | Yes |
| Configure agents, repositories, and automations | No | Yes | Yes |
| Attach existing secrets, MCP servers, and integrations | No | Yes | Yes |
| Manage factory roles | No | No | Yes |
| Delete the factory | No | No | Yes |

Admin access comes from your [team or workspace role](https://docs.warp.dev/enterprise/team-management/roles-and-permissions/). You cannot assign Admin through a factory role dropdown or reduce an admin's access with a User override.

Attaching an existing resource is different from managing the shared resource. Connecting team-wide integrations and managing shared runners require separate team or workspace permissions.

## How roles apply

A person's team-level Warp Factories role applies across the team's factories. A per-factory override changes their role for one factory.

For example, give a teammate User access across the team, then Editor access to the payments factory they maintain. They can configure payments without changing other factories.

For a non-admin Enterprise team member, Warp resolves the role in this order:

| Priority | Role source |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | The person's override for this factory |
| 2 | The person's team-level Warp Factories role |
| 3 | The applicable workspace or team default |
| 4 | Editor, if no role or default is set |

The workspace default takes precedence over the team default when the workspace sets one. A default applies to new and existing members who have no assigned role. Changing a default does not replace a person's explicit team role or per-factory override.

Factory creation uses the team-level role, not a per-factory override. Team-level Editors and team or workspace admins can create factories. Editor access to one existing factory does not grant factory creation.

## Changing a team-level role

You must be a team or workspace admin to assign User and Editor roles.

1. In the <a href={VARS.FACTORY_WEB_APP_URL}>{VARS.FACTORY_WEB_APP}</a>, open a factory and click **Settings** > **Access**.
2. Click **Manage team access**.
3. In the teammate's row, click their current seat type.
4. Choose **Factory user** or **Editor**, then click **Save**.

The role applies across the team's factories, except where the teammate has a per-factory override.

## Changing a role for one factory

As a team or workspace admin, give a teammate a different role in one factory:

1. In the web app, open that factory and click **Settings** > **Access**.
2. Find the teammate by name or email. In their role dropdown, choose **Factory user** or **Editor**.
3. Click **Save changes**.

The new role applies only to this factory. It does not change the teammate's team-level role or their access to other factories.

## Factory permissions and external access

Factory roles govern who runs and configures the factory. They do not grant access to additional repositories or services. Each run uses the factory's configured [repository identity](/factories/factory-as-code/#credentialstrategy), [secrets](/factories/secrets/), and [MCP servers](/factories/factory-as-code/).

Specification approval follows the foreman's instructions. Your repository's permissions and branch protection control who can approve and merge pull requests. See [Human decision points and permissions](/factories/factory-agents/#human-decision-points-and-permissions).
