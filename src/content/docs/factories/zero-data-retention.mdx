Provider Zero Data Retention (ZDR) requires AI providers not to retain your prompts and responses. For factory inference, coverage depends on the model and whose provider account serves the request, not where the agent runs commands.

Warp's [provider ZDR policy](/support-and-community/privacy-and-security/privacy/#zero-data-retention-zdr) applies to supported models on every plan when Warp supplies the provider account. This is called Warp-managed inference.

<span id="choose-inference-covered-by-provider-zdr" />

## Provider ZDR coverage

See [inference options for factory runs](/factories/infrastructure-and-security/#inference-options-for-factory-runs) for supported providers, credentials, and harnesses. Personal keys and endpoints stored on your device do not apply to factory runs.

* **Warp-managed inference** - Avoid [models that require provider retention](/agents/inference/model-choice/#models-requiring-provider-retention). Apply that choice to each agent and any separate model settings or overrides, not only the foreman.
* **Your provider account** - For team-managed keys and endpoints, BYOLLM, or direct Claude Code or Codex calls, you'll need to verify ZDR with the provider for your specific account, model, and configuration. Warp's agreements don't establish ZDR for those requests, even if you avoid the listed models. If your endpoint routes to other providers, verify their retention policies too.

Some factory features use models other than the agent's selected model. Avoiding models that require retention does not by itself confirm ZDR coverage for every AI request. If your organization requires that coverage, contact Warp before sending sensitive work to your factory.

## What Warp collects and stores

Provider ZDR governs what AI providers retain. Warp's improvement collection and storage of factory run data are separate.

### Improvement collection

The **Help improve Warp** toggle controls Warp's analytics and eligible content collection for service improvement. It doesn't change provider retention.

For team content collection, follow the [browser procedure in the Admin Panel](/enterprise/team-management/admin-panel/#changing-improvement-collection-in-a-browser), including plan and workspace restrictions. Personal analytics and crash preferences are separate. See [analytics and storage controls](/support-and-community/privacy-and-security/privacy/#provider-retention-analytics-and-storage).

### Run history

Warp stores transcripts, prompt attachments, and uploaded artifacts so you can revisit runs, even with analytics off. Destroying a sandbox doesn't delete these records.

Enterprise admins can configure [self-hosted storage](/factories/self-hosting/data-storage/) separately for transcripts, prompt attachments, and artifacts. Categories without a self-hosted storage configuration remain with Warp.

Self-hosting execution does not establish provider ZDR or move saved run data. The **Store AI conversations in the cloud** toggle controls personal Terminal conversations, not factory run storage.

## Reviewing your factory's inference settings

Self-serve teams can change models and overrides manually, but don't have a team-wide model-blocking control. Business supports shared provider keys and endpoints. Enterprise adds [model restrictions in the Admin Panel](/enterprise/team-management/admin-panel/#models-settings).

Choose the procedure for where your definition is managed. Both work in a browser without installing Warp Terminal and include checks for defaults, overrides, and new runs:

* [Reviewing a dashboard-managed factory](/factories/factory-agents/configuration/#reviewing-a-dashboard-managed-factory)
* [Reviewing a GitHub-backed factory](/factories/factory-agents/configuration/#reviewing-a-github-backed-factory)
